# UbuntuCare: single private hospital workflow and pilot roadmap

Prepared 15 November 2026. This is a product and implementation recommendation based on a read-only review of the synthetic demo and the primary sources linked below. The proposed workflows and numerical pilot targets are design recommendations, not statutory service levels or a claim that this prototype is clinically validated.

## The product to sell first

Sell a **single hospital coordination and patient administration pilot** covering pre-admission, forms, appointments, staff handoffs, patient concerns, discharge follow-up and records requests. Choose one elective service line and one ward. Keep the hospital's existing clinical, pharmacy, diagnostic and financial systems as the agreed sources of truth until each replacement or interface has been separately validated.

The buyer receives a shared work queue with a named owner, due time, status, patient/encounter reference, evidence and history for every handoff. That addresses a concrete problem: staff repeatedly chase forms, appointments, signatures, records and responses across paper, phone calls and inboxes. A hospital-wide platform can grow from the same workflow primitives, but promising to replace every departmental system in a first pilot creates avoidable integration and clinical risk.

The recommended patient journey is:

`Referral/appointment or EMS arrival → registration and identity reconciliation → consent and admission pack → triage/bed assignment → clinician and departmental worklists → results/review → clinician-authorised discharge → patient instructions and follow-up → records/referral completion → billing and reconciliation`

Funding review proceeds alongside care. An unavailable verification service, missing identity document, declined authorisation or unpaid account must enter an exception queue with a human owner; emergency treatment must not depend on those checks. Section 5 of the National Health Act prohibits refusal of emergency treatment. [National Health Act, section 5](https://www.health.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/NHREC-NHA-of-2003.pdf)

## What the inspected demo already proves, and what it does not

The repository at `/workspace/work/ubuntu-care-sa-demo-v4` already persists a synthetic EMS-to-discharge encounter lifecycle, clinical notes, observations, lab order/review, medication orders/administrations, seven roles, consent choices, audit events and simulated identity, eligibility, authorisation, coordination calls and claims. Its encounter and claim services use server-side roles, encounter scope, state prerequisites, record versions and transactional audit writes. Provider references are explicitly simulated; there are no live external integrations.

The legacy workspace also has appointment, scan preview, theatre, transfer, document, email-register, staff message and task modules. These are useful demonstrations, not complete department implementations. At inspection time:

- `DocumentRecord` held category/title/body/author, with no document version, signing evidence, publication lifecycle or amendment history.
- `MessageRecord` held a channel and body without a patient, recipient or thread model. A general channel cannot safely serve as patient-specific clinical messaging without explicit participant and record scoping.
- `EmailLog` recorded an intended recipient and status; it did not prove delivery, receipt or reading.
- Appointment updates accepted a status string and a bare record ID; production scheduling also needs patient/department scope, allowed transitions, concurrent-edit protection, slot/resource rules and cancellations.
- The README explicitly identifies incomplete audit coverage on legacy modules, no patient invitation/enrolment flow, no closed-encounter amendments and no clinical validation. New fictional registration does not establish patient account ownership.
- A scan preview is not a diagnostic PACS viewer; a prescription record is not pharmacy dispensing or stock control; a theatre booking is not a complete perioperative record; a JSON claim export is not a clearinghouse connection.

Re-check these findings against the current branch before implementation because other work may change the legacy modules. Relevant files: `README.md`, `docs/INTEGRATIONS.md`, `prisma/schema.prisma`, `lib/care-access.ts`, `app/api/appointments/route.ts`, `app/api/documents/route.ts` and `app/api/messages/route.ts`.

## Cross-department design rules

Use one stable hospital patient identifier with documented external identifier mappings and one encounter/episode reference. Duplicate patient merges require trained staff and an auditable decision. Link each work item, clinical entry, document, appointment and message to its appropriate patient and episode; administrative tasks may instead link to a department or location.

Each workflow requires a status machine, accountable team/person, priority, created/updated/due timestamps, escalation route, evidence and version. Separate a request being created, another department accepting it, completion and recipient acknowledgement. Prevent duplicate effects through idempotency keys and explicit message/provider reference IDs. A due date without a staffed escalation owner is not an operational workflow.

Access must combine role, facility, department, care-team relationship, patient ownership and specific release purpose. The demo's broad doctor/nurse access is not a complete production care-team model. Add separate roles for pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, theatre, records and patient experience as those modules become operational. Insurer users receive the minimal submitted claim packet; they do not inherit patient-chart access. Emergency access needs a logged reason, time limit and retrospective review.

Make UTC the stored timestamp and `Africa/Johannesburg` the displayed hospital zone, with explicit offsets in exports. Test staff browsers configured to other zones. Use durable job scheduling for reminders, a transactional outbox for live integrations, bounded retries and reconciliation/dead-letter worklists. UI refresh timers or a browser tab are not reliable job runners.

Store uploaded documents outside web-public directories using private object storage, malware/content validation, size/type limits and access-controlled downloads. Preserve original and amended documents, provenance and retention/legal holds. A screenshot or scan without indexing and patient reconciliation does not complete records migration.

## Verified South African standards and practical consequences

1. **Health records and hospital operations.** The 2018 health-establishment norms cover confidentiality and managed records, formal informed consent, referral documents retained in the health record, discharge reports, clinical risk, medicines, diagnostics and adverse events. Product consequence: these are owned workflows with evidence, rather than isolated form screens. Applicability must be checked against the establishment's relevant assessment tools and hospital policies. [NDoH norms and standards, regulations 4–7 and contents](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201802/41419gon67.pdf)

2. **POPIA is more than a consent checkbox.** The Act provides different authorisations for processing, including health information, and safeguards/operational duties. Map the actual lawful basis, necessity, sharing purpose, controller/operator responsibilities and confidentiality obligations for each use. Treatment consent, information-sharing permission, billing authorisation and channel preference should be distinct records. Withdrawal of an optional permission does not mean clinical records or lawful retention duties disappear. [Protection of Personal Information Act, especially sections 19–22, 27, 32 and 72](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/InfoRegSA-act-2013-004.pdf)

3. **A relevant 2026 update.** Notice 7198, Gazette 54268, published 6 March 2026, regulates health-information processing by specified parties, including medical schemes, administrators, insurers and relevant operators. It addresses safeguards, confidentiality and cross-border transfers and commenced on publication. Its listed scope must be assessed for the hospital's insurer-facing relationships; it should not be described as automatically applying to every hospital activity. Include it in the legal/information-officer review of claims, cloud hosting and operator contracts. [2026 health-information regulations, regulations 3–7](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202603/54268gon7198.pdf)

4. **Interoperability baseline.** The 2021 HNSF, gazetted on 21 October 2022, supersedes previous versions and covers private-sector information systems. It uses specific IHE profiles and underlying standards, including HL7/FHIR, document sharing, patient identity and DICOM-related uses. Select the use case, profile/version and partner contract before implementing an interface. A generic JSON API or use of a FHIR resource name is not evidence of conformance. [NDoH 2021 HNSF, gazetted 2022](https://www.health.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/HNSF_Gazette_21_October_2022.pdf)

5. **Professional practice.** The HPCSA's official guidance index links informed consent, confidentiality, patient records, telehealth and ethical billing. Use the current applicable booklets during clinical workflow approval, including record retention/amendments and telehealth consent/recording policy. The linked booklet PDFs were unavailable through the research browser, so this report does not assert precise retention periods from an unverified copy. [HPCSA guidance index](https://www.hpcsa-blogs.co.za/new-and-updated-ethical-guidelines/)

6. **Electronic signing needs classification.** ECTA section 13 distinguishes signature requirements imposed by law from signatures required by parties; identity, approval and appropriate reliability matter. The legal/form owner must classify each form and approve the signing method and evidence, including advanced electronic signatures where applicable. Keep the prototype's typed-name workflow labelled as a simulated acknowledgement. Do not market it as making every consent or prescription legally valid. [ECTA, sections 12–16](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a25-02.pdf)

7. **Prescribing and dispensing need pharmacist governance.** SAPC's current official publication discusses electronic prescription integrity, authenticity, direct verified transmission, verification and repeated dispensing risk. Preserve the separate pharmacist check and validate the actual prescribing/dispensing integration before live use. The publication contains an apparent cross-reference date inconsistency, so verify implementation details with the responsible pharmacist and underlying current regulations. [SAPC Pharmaciae: electronic transmission of prescriptions](https://pharmaciae.co.za/electronic-transmission-of-prescriptions/)

8. **Funding integration is provider-specific.** MediKredit describes hospital HIS-to-switch eligibility, authorisation verification, adjudication/rejection and billing write-back. This is evidence of a possible contracted integration route, not proof of public API access or support for every scheme. Record scheme, administrator and switch separately; obtain each partner's contract, payload specification, test environment, supported transactions and reconciliation rules. [MediKredit hospital claims processing](https://dsp.medikredit.co.za/article/medikredit_s_hospital_claims_processing_functionality)

9. **Medical scheme and administrator are distinct.** Discovery's provider page identifies Discovery Health Medical Scheme as a separate entity administered by Discovery Health (Pty) Ltd. Scheme-specific claims requirements differ; a simple insurer account in the demo is not the complete real claims organisation model. [Discovery healthcare-professional information](https://www.discovery.co.za/medical-aid/for-healthcare-professionals)

10. **Benefit rules need evidence.** CMS describes PMBs and their relationship to diagnosis and designated service providers. Use qualified coding/benefit review and scheme-specific rules. Eligibility or an authorisation reference alone must not be shown as a guarantee that the final invoice will be paid. [Council for Medical Schemes: PMBs](https://www.medicalschemes.co.za/resources/pmb/)

11. **Practical private-hospital precedent.** Netcare provides online pre-admission documentation; its discharge information describes ward documents and follow-up booking. These support the practicality of a focused administration/coordination pilot, not evidence that this product can connect to Netcare or copy its full workflow. [Netcare pre-admission](https://www.netcare.co.za/netcare-hospitals/patient-information/hospital-pre-admission), [Netcare discharge and follow-up](https://www.netcare.co.za/netcare-hospitals/patient-information/hospital-discharge/before-you-leave)

## Human escalation and assistant boundaries

Use assistants for narrow, reviewable work: detect missing forms, draft a reminder, summarise a thread with source links, classify a records request for human review, suggest a task owner or prepare a discharge checklist from approved entries. Prefer deterministic rules for deadlines, required fields, permissions, document state and escalation. Identify each suggestion as draft/assistant-produced, store the input source references and show what a human changed.

Assistants must not independently diagnose, downgrade triage, prescribe/change/administer medicines, interpret imaging as a clinician, approve blood products, declare clinical fitness, discharge a patient, accept a claim as payable, release records, impersonate a signer or send an external communication without the workflow's authorised action. A payer's funding response must never make the care decision. The treating team remains accountable for clinical decisions.

Every queue needs a primary owner, staffed hours, deputy/duty team, locally approved acknowledgement/response targets and an overdue route. For a concern suggesting immediate deterioration, show the hospital's immediate help route and alert the responsible clinical team according to policy; do not imply an asynchronous portal is continuously monitored or that an AI has triaged it safely. Routine complaints, billing queries and clinical concerns need distinct routing. A patient-visible reference and escalation status should survive reassignment or shift change.

Before using an external AI provider with real records, agree the processing purpose, minimum fields, hosting/transfers, retention, training-use restrictions, operator contract, access and incident handling. Treat imported PDFs, messages and records as untrusted content: instructions inside them cannot grant tools, change recipients or bypass record access. Bound assistants to the current patient/encounter and allowed tools; validate outputs against structured records and deny unsupported actions server-side.

An assistant failure returns work to the human queue. Require review of uncertain identity matches, conflicting records, clinical wording, absent sources and proposed external recipients. Preserve a plain manual workflow so work can continue during AI/provider outages. Deterministic draft/template helpers in the demo should be labelled as such; do not imply an LLM is connected when it is not.

## Pilot business validation and metrics

Interview and observe admissions, ward nursing, outpatient reception, records, patient experience, billing and IT/security before setting the commercial scope. Include one specialist/practice manager because independent consulting rooms may control appointments and billing outside hospital systems. Ask each team to supply its top repeated handoffs, weekly volumes, current chase time, failure reasons, actual decision rights and authoritative records. Have the hospital manager/CFO identify the budget and the owner who can accept the service.

The paid pilot statement of work should name one hospital, ward/service line, cohort and volume limit; included forms/workflows and roles; existing systems/interfaces; training and support hours; data ownership/export/exit; integration charges; changes outside scope; success measures and acceptance evidence. Set the price only after workflow and integration discovery; no reliable hospital-specific budget or procurement timetable has been established here. Separate setup/configuration, recurring support/software and optional integration work so benefits and costs can be evaluated honestly.

Suggested starting scorecard, to be agreed after a baseline period:

| Measure | Definition | Initial proposed target / decision use |
|---|---|---|
| Administration effort | Median observed staff minutes per completed admission pack, including chasing and re-entry | Aim for at least 25% reduction in the selected workflow, measured with comparable case mix; do not claim realised savings before measurement. |
| Form completeness | Required approved forms complete by locally agreed milestone / eligible encounters | At least 95%; track refusal/clinical emergency exceptions separately. |
| Departmental handoff | Accepted tasks / tasks requiring another team's acceptance; median and 90th percentile acceptance time | At least 95% accepted within the hospital-agreed target; no unowned overdue safety task. |
| Scheduling | Confirmed appointments with correct patient/resource/time; cancellation and no-show rates | Zero unexplained wrong-patient/slot events; compare no-shows to baseline with sufficient volume before attributing benefit. |
| Patient concerns | Acknowledged/resolved within local target; overdue safety escalations | 100% of tested safety escalations reach an accountable human; routine targets set with staffed hours. |
| Records requests | Median request-to-authorised-release time; incomplete/returned requests | Improve time versus baseline without any unauthorised release; quantify reasons for delay. |
| Communication | Live delivered messages / attempted messages, with failures visible; simulated runs reported separately | No unresolved silent failures or duplicate sends in acceptance scenarios; real target set with provider SLA. |
| Traceability and isolation | Audited consequential mutations; negative access tests; wrong-patient incidents | 100% of specified consequential actions auditable; all negative access tests pass; any suspected disclosure pauses affected workflow pending review. |
| Adoption and burden | Eligible cases completed in the workflow; staff active use; duplicate entry; support minutes per case | At least 80% of eligible cases by the end of supported pilot; burden/support trending down, with reasons for non-use recorded. |
| Financial benefit, later phase | Avoidable query/return rate and time to accepted claim/remittance reconciliation | Compare with scheme/administrator and case mix matched baseline; no target that encourages unsupported coding or unsafe discharge. |

Review the scorecard weekly with the operational owners, including failures and denominator counts. At pilot end, calculate measured staff capacity released and avoided rework against setup, subscription, messaging, integration and support costs. Expansion requires both a credible buyer case and department acceptance; an attractive demo alone does not establish demand, safety, compliance or return on investment.
